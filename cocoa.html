ABOUT THE PROJECT

A statistical forecasting project combining historical cocoa prices with temperature and rainfall observations from Ghana. The study compares price-only models with weather and lag-based approaches, examining what they capture—and where forecasts fall short during sharp market shifts.

THE RESEARCH QUESTION

More context.
Better forecasts?

Test whether adding weather, trend and recent price history improves on models built from prices alone.

01 / ANALYTICAL PROCESS

From observations
to model comparison.

  1. 01

    Prepare & align

    Standardize dates and price values, handle missing observations, and join price and weather records by date.

  2. 02

    Inspect the time series

    Explore price movements and weather patterns. Use ACF and PACF to examine dependence across time.

  3. 03

    Build competing specifications

    Start with ETS and ARIMA. Add weather regressors, a seasonal specification and trend; then test log-price regression with seven lag features.

  4. 04

    Evaluate & diagnose

    Compare reported MAE, RMSE and MAPE. Inspect residual and Q–Q plots, and contrast predictions with the observed price surge.

02 / WHAT THE REPORT FOUND

Trend helped.
The surge remained difficult.

The report’s trend-augmented specification reduced error, but still substantially underestimated the sharp rise in prices.

WEATHER SIGNAL

Additional inputs did not guarantee improvement.

ARIMA and weather-augmented ARIMAX had almost identical reported errors. This comparison does not establish a causal effect of climate on prices.

FORECAST DESIGN

A strong fit needs a fair comparison.

Lag-based regression reported much lower error. Its forecast horizon and use of observed lagged prices need verification before treating it as superior to multi-step forecasts.

03 / INTERPRETING THE EVIDENCE

What needs checking next.

The report contains inconsistent descriptions of the train/test split and ETS results. A reproducible follow-up should verify date ordering, train-only imputation, weather units and forecast-time feature availability, then compare every model on the same rolling forecast horizon.
